| F21 | **The water-filling law: three closed forms replace the capped-shifting LP and explain F3, F4, F7 and F15.** With 24 h slack, flexible work fills each day's cleanest hours at its maximum rate phi + h. (a) *Homogeneity theorem:* the LP is homogeneous in (phi, h), so the share of the ideal saving kept depends only on h/phi, the headroom per unit of flexible load; verified exactly (max difference 0.0000 over 1,200 zone-level pairs). (b) *Closed form:* saving = phi x A x sinc(pi phi/(phi+h)), with A the mean daily (mean - min) of the signal; uncapped saving = phi x A (R² 0.998); capped, R² 0.968 against 4,680 LP solves, median error 0.9%. (c) *Value of a kW* = the daily area between the 'water level' (price of the marginal filled hour) and the cheaper filled hours: R² 0.981 and median ratio 1.002 against 360 LP shadow prices; for a sinusoidal day it is pi A q² abs(sinc'(pi q)), linear in amplitude and independent of the mean, which derives F15's swing law. (d) *Peak draw* of the uncapped schedule = 1 + slack x phi exactly (13x for phi = 0.5, F3) | `src/carbonshift/waterfill.py`, `tests/test_waterfill.py`, `scripts/waterfill_experiments.py`, `results/waterfill_*.csv` | verified; 24 h slack only, 2022 for (b), 2020-22 for (c) |
| F22 | **A flexible workload under a power cap *is* a lossless battery, and this one picture unifies the facility, battery and signal results.** Theorem: with backlog B = arrivals - served, the cumulative-flow constraints become 0 <= B <= s x phi and -h <= dB <= phi, so capped shifting equals a battery of energy s x phi (12 h of mean load for phi = 0.5, 24 h), discharge power phi, charge power h and 100% efficiency; only the boundary condition differs (gap O(1/days); median 0.14% on 20 real zones, max 0.22 g/kWh on flat hydro grids). Consequences, all tested: (a) *effective horizon:* the virtual battery refills in s x phi / h hours, so tight caps plan beyond the nominal slack (10 days at +5% headroom); water-filling over T = s x max(1, phi/h) cuts the tight-cap bias of fixed blocks from -38% to -4.6% (24 h slack) and from -24% to -1.6% (168 h), R² 0.984 / 0.991 against all LP results; (b) *F5 explained:* a 10-min UPS is a battery with 1/72 of the workload's virtual energy and 90% efficiency; (c) *F19 vs F20 explained:* a lossless 1 h battery dispatched on 0.7 x average + 0.3 x marginal worsens neither ledger in any of 48 BAs (0% / 0%), while the same battery at 90% round trip worsens the average ledger in 60%: round-trip loss, not storage, is what prevents serving both ledgers | `scripts/virtual_battery_experiments.py`, `results/virtual_battery_*.csv`, `src/carbonshift/waterfill.py` (`saving_general`), `tests/test_waterfill.py::test_capped_shift_is_a_lossless_battery` | verified; flat load, h <= phi for the battery mapping in code (the theorem holds for any h) |
