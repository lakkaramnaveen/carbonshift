## 2. The strongest single-paper story

**Full paper: "Your Data Centre Is Already a Battery: A Closed-Form Theory of Carbon-Aware Shifting under Power Caps."**
This is the deepest original contribution and has one idea throughout:

1. *Problem* (F3, F4, F12): the EuroSys'24 upper bounds need 13x peak draw. Real facilities have about +42% headroom and keep about half the ideal saving.
2. *Theory* (F21, F22): the water-filling law (sinc closed form, homogeneity in h/φ, water-level shadow price) and the theorem that capped flexible load is a lossless battery. Together they explain F3, F4, F5, F7 and F15 as corollaries.
3. *Price* (F23): the exchange rate against real batteries (4.3 kWh per kW at +20%), plus embodied carbon (F8, F16).
4. *Boundary of the theory* (F29, new): not every knob is a battery. Deferral is linear in utilisation, so its saving is first order in the daily swing; carbon-aware speed scaling (DVFS, GPU power limits) is convex, so its saving is the power-mean gap 1 - M_p(c)/M_1(c), second order in CV (a Harberger triangle): a median 0.79% vs 14.6% uncapped. This gives the paper a sharp, quotable contrast ("the carbon knob is *when*, not *how fast*") and pre-empts the reviewer question "why not just throttle?".
5. *Fleet consequence* (F24): the power cap is an anti-herding device.
5b. *Two batteries on one grid* (F32, new in run 34): if flexible load is a battery, it competes with the grid's batteries. No-arbitrage theorem: where grid storage is unconstrained, m_charge = η m_discharge, so shifting saves only (1 - η) of today's value. On CAISO, the 2025 battery build alone (+4.7 GW / 16.5 GWh) leaves 41-45% of the 2024 real saving; unlimited storage leaves (1 - η) + 0.035. This closes the battery paper with a forward-looking result: the carbon value of flexible compute is being competed away on the grids that build storage fastest, and becomes a storage-capex value (F23). Drafted as §'Two Batteries on One Grid' in `main.tex` with theorem, proof, figure (vector PDF).
5c. *The battery has a standby load* (F33, new in run 35): the virtual battery needs spare servers, and spare servers that stay on draw idle power. Always-on capacity built for shifting pays only if the idle share is below p0*(h) = v/(v + c̄); the median break-even at +42% is 10%, against a SPECpower x86 mean of 25.7%, so it raises emissions in 98% of zones. This bounds the theory from the hardware side, as F29 bounds it from the knob side and F32 from the grid side, and gives a practical rule: shift only onto capacity that sleeps. Drafted as §'The Battery Has a Standby Load' (proposition, proof, vector figure).
5d. *The price of a shifted tonne* (F34, new in run 36): the economic boundary. Priced in money, headroom built for shifting abates at a median $3,558/t (facility power, +42%) and $33,000/t (GPU capacity), against an SCC of $190/t, in no zone below it; a horizon-free floor (v <= c_max - c̄) shows GPU capacity can never pass on any grid. This turns the paper's message from "a free battery" into "a battery you should never buy, only use when you already own it", closing the boundary trio (knob: F29; grid: F32; hardware: F33; money: F34). Drafted as §'The Price of a Shifted Tonne' (proposition, appendix proof, vector figure `fig_abatement.pdf`). It also fits the *Moved, Not Cut* workshop as a fourth reason the reported saving overstates the social value.
5e. *Who produced the saving* (F35, new in run 37): Euler's theorem on the homogeneous, concave S(φ, h) splits the saving exactly between users' flexibility and operators' capacity (the Aumann-Shapley value). Capacity produces a median 55% at +20% and 43% at +42% (exact LP, 120 zones, stable over 3 years), and the split is set by how clock-like the grid is (Spearman 0.82), not by its swing. It ties the boundaries together: the input that produces the saving on solar/clock-like grids is the input whose idle power (F33) and price (F34) bound it. It also gives the paper a practical corollary: per-job carbon claims overstate the job's counterfactual by about 2×. Drafted as §'Who Produced the Saving?' (proposition, appendix proof via supergradients, vector figure `fig_attribution.pdf`). A natural bridge to the *Moved, Not Cut* workshop paper (who gets the credit, F28/F30).
5f. *Which signal* (F36, new in run 38): the measurement boundary. Every number in the paper is on one carbon signal. Correlation law: a schedule optimised on signal A keeps ρ of signal B's optimum (ρ = correlation of within-day shapes) and its claim is confirmed at β = ρ σ_B/σ_A (the optimiser's curse in regression form). On 44 US BAs the two average methodologies (flow-traced Electricity Maps vs production-based EIA-930) agree on *when* (ρ 0.90, ~90% kept) but not on *how much* (11/44 BAs off by >1.5x); over 264 signal pairs, including average vs marginal, kept = 1.01ρ + 0.002, R² 0.93. It pre-empts the reviewer question "which signal did you use, and does it matter?" with a one-number answer, and it turns F17 (average vs marginal) into a special case. Drafted as §'Which Signal? The Correlation Law' (proposition, appendix proof, vector figure `fig_provenance.pdf`). Also a strong addition to *Moved, Not Cut*: the reported magnitude is methodology-relative before any accounting question is asked.
6. *Validation*: 4,680 LP solves, 3 years, out-of-year checks; power-mean law exact against the optimiser and a generic solver.

**Workshop (or short e-Energy) paper: "Moved, Not Cut: Who Bears the Cost of Carbon-Aware Computing?"** (F28 + F30 + F31, with F17, F19 and F20). *New in run 33 (F31):* the third "moved" mechanism. Under an emissions cap, an avoided tonne is moved through the allowance bank unless the EU's Market Stability Reserve absorbs it, and it absorbs only while the 2025 TNAC (1,023 Mt) stays in the 833-1,096 Mt band. That ends within about a year of the market turning short. 41% of the cloud-weighted shifting saving sits under a cap. The paper now has one argument in three acts: grid average (F28), hourly matching (F30), cap (F31). Draft §7 is written. This is the result most likely to change how people think, and F30 makes it much stronger: location-based savings on most US grids are transfers onto other consumers (83%), and the stricter standard now proposed, hourly matching / 24/7 CFE, is *worse* for shifting (94% transfer, reported savings 5.6x larger). One rule fixes both: the two-part rule with portfolios credited at the margin. It is timely because the GHG Protocol's October 2026 status update still has hourly matching ('Method 1') under consideration. Draft: `scripts/build_workshop_incidence.py` builds a 3-page double-blind PDF (§6 is F30). With F30 the story could also stand as a full e-Energy 2027 paper if the related work on 24/7 CFE (Riepin et al. 2025; Xu et al. 2024) is read in full and a non-US check is added.

The other drafts are kept for reuse: `build_workshop.py` (signals, F17/F19/F20; superseded by the incidence draft), `build_workshop_battery.py` (F21-F23; the seed of the full paper) and `build_workshop_cooling.py` (F26-F27; a BuildSys/e-Energy candidate).
