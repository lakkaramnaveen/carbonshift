| F11 | Forecast error and power caps compound almost independently at realistic error. At 5-15% MAPE (CarbonCast range), +20% headroom keeps 35-38% of the equally mis-forecast uncapped saving (36% with perfect forecasts). Only at 30% MAPE does the cap hedge (keeps 52%), because capped schedules spread work over many hours. Uncapped forecast error costs 0.8-2.9% of emissions, consistent with [1]. Shifting almost never backfires | `results/forecast_*.csv`, `src/carbonshift/forecast.py`, `tests/test_forecast.py` | verified (synthetic AR(1) errors, rho = 0.9) |
| F12 | Real Google power domains (PowerData2019, 57 domains) run at a median 70% of power capacity (52-78%), i.e. +42% headroom. At their real headroom, carbon shifting keeps a median 54% of the ideal saving (44-75%). The daily load shape barely matters: the worst alignment of the fleet-median shape with the grid loses 1.4% of the saving, and the most diurnal domain (21% swing) loses 5.9%; averaged over alignments it equals the flat model | `results/diurnal_*.csv`, `src/carbonshift/google_power.py`, `tests/test_facility.py::test_varying_load_equals_brute_force` | verified (one month, 2019; utilisation includes cooling) |
| F13 | Spatial and temporal flexibility want capacity in different places. Across 32 GCP regions (Google-real 70% utilisation, phi = 0.5), +1 kW in Quebec is worth 2,943 kg CO2/yr for migration at a 100 ms limit, 22x the median temporal value (137). Spatial value tracks low mean intensity (Spearman -0.84 at 100 ms, -0.97 at 250 ms); temporal value tracks daily variability (0.58). The two rankings are negatively correlated (-0.39 to -0.15), so siting depends on which flexibility the workload has | `results/siting_*.csv`, `src/carbonshift/spatial_capacity.py`, `tests/test_spatial_capacity.py` | verified under average CI; marginal emissions of hydro regions may be much higher |
| F14 | Under a latency budget, static batching at the efficient size cuts LLM inference CO2 by 41-71% versus batch 1 (measured M4 Pro curves); carbon-aware, time-varying batching adds only 1.6-4.8% more (pooled over 120 grids), up to 24% in very variable grids (Uruguay, Bornholm). Batch sizes 8 and 16 are dominated by 32 (lower energy, nearly the same per-token latency) | `results/llm_batching_*.csv`, `src/carbonshift/llm_batching.py` (Lagrangian solution, tested equal to the LP) | verified on one device and two models |
| F15 | The carbon value of capacity is set by the grid's *absolute* daily swing, not its level. Lemma: for ci' = a + b*ci, schedules are unchanged and savings and shadow prices scale by b (tested). Empirically (120 zones x 3 years), shadow price vs daily std: Spearman 0.993; vs mean 0.55; vs CV 0.48. One-statistic predictor: value ≈ 4.83 x std^1.12 kg/kW-yr (R² 0.96). The 2020->2022 change in value follows the change in swing (ρ 0.97) and is unrelated to the change in mean (ρ -0.01); the median swing rose 15%, explaining F10. Decarbonisation raises capacity value only if it widens swings | `results/variability_*.csv`, `scripts/variability_drivers.py` | verified |
| F16 | F8 survives embodied-carbon uncertainty. Monte Carlo (5,000 draws over EPD ranges, server draw 0.25-0.5 kW, GPU full-server multiplier 1.5-3x, lifetimes, 0-5%/yr decline): the build ordering CPU < GPU <= backed <= grid-only holds in 100% of draws. At +20% headroom, electrical builds repay in 92-98% of zones in every draw; CPU servers in a median 23% (90% interval 1-52%), with a majority of zones in only 5% of draws. At +50% headroom even GPU servers are a coin flip (median 50%) | `results/embodied_uncertainty_summary.csv`, `scripts/embodied_uncertainty.py` | verified |
| F17 | Under a *marginal* signal (EIA-930, 48 US balancing authorities, Siler-Evans season x hour MEFs): (a) the swing law (F15) holds, with shadow price vs marginal daily std ρ = 0.96 and vs mean ρ = 0.16; (b) a capped schedule optimised on the *average* signal captures only 7% of the marginal-optimal saving (median 1.5%) and *raises* marginal emissions in 44% of BAs; (c) siting rankings by mean intensity agree at ρ = 0.78 with notable reversals (e.g. SRP, DUK). Validation: EIA-derived averages vs Electricity Maps r = 0.91 (annual means), 0.88 (hourly median) | `results/marginal_*.csv`, `src/carbonshift/eia930.py`, `tests/test_eia930.py` | verified; bin-level MEFs are coarse and partly noise |
| F18 | Under real per-region caps, spatial and temporal flexibility compete for the same headroom. Europe / US GCP regions (100 ms, 24 h slack): temporal alone saves 5.4% / 5.1%, but adds only 0.8 / 0.7 pp on top of migration (86-86% of its value lost). With unlimited capacity it still adds 3.0 / 6.7 pp. Interaction ratio (joint / sum): 0.84 / 0.76 real vs 0.86 / 0.93 unlimited | `results/joint_*.csv`, `spatial_capacity.joint_shift` (reduction-tested) | verified |

## Iteration notes

- **Iteration 1 (2026-11-09):** multi-year robustness check (F10). No novelty search was needed, since this is a validity check. One definition was corrected (share kept = ratio of mean absolute savings, matching `facility_summary.csv`).
- **Iteration 2 (2026-11-09):** forecast error under a power cap (F11). Novelty check: prior work quantifies forecast-error loss for uncapped shifting (arXiv:2508.14625; FM-CAC arXiv:2604.16448), but I found none for power-capped facilities. The headline metric was cross-checked against [1]'s 'about 3% of emissions' at CarbonCast-level error.
- **Iteration 3 (2026-11-09):** real facility load from Google PowerData2019 (F12). Novelty check: carbon-aware work (CICS, VCC) uses internal Google data; I found no public study that combines the released power traces with cross-grid carbon data. The documented meaning of `bad_measurement_data` contradicts the data (24 of 508,896 rows are True), so True rows were treated as bad. The library now supports time-varying load (brute-force tested).
- **Iteration 4 (2026-11-09):** siting capacity across a cloud's regions (F13). Novelty check: UMass CDN capacity shifting (SoCC'24) and CarbonEdge (HPDC'25, Wu, Hanafy, Souza et al.) study shifting load or capacity between sites; I found no work that ranks regions for new capacity by spatial and temporal shadow price. Latency-file codes AUS-NSW/AUS-VIC were mapped to AU-NSW/AU-VIC (32 regions).
- **Iteration 5 (2026-11-09):** carbon-aware LLM batching (F14). Novelty check: Hoxha et al. (preprint, Oct 2025) jointly pick region and batch per prompt; I found no measurement-based estimate of the *time-varying* batching gain over the best static batching across many grids. The first full-LP implementation was too slow (70k variables x 1,100 solves), so it was replaced by an exact Lagrangian bisection, tested equal to the LP.
- **Iteration 6 (2026-11-09):** what drives capacity value (F15). This answers the decarbonisation-trajectory item analytically: by the affine-invariance lemma, only absolute swing matters, so no synthetic renewable scenario is needed. Novelty: [1] uses CV (relative variability) as its indicator for temporal shifting; for capacity value the right indicator is absolute std, which beats CV by a wide margin (0.99 vs 0.48).
- **Loop stopped (2026-11-09):** the remaining backlog item cannot be done with free data. Six iterations added F10-F15, 4 modules, 6 experiment scripts, 16 tests and 5 paper sections.
- **Loop restarted (2026-11-09):** the user asked to keep going. Backlog refilled: EIA-930 offers free marginal-emission data for US balancing authorities, which reopens the deferred marginal-signals item for US regions.
- **Iteration 7 (2026-11-09):** embodied-carbon Monte Carlo (F16). Robustness check, so no novelty search. Vectorised payback tested against the scalar version.
- **Iteration 8 (2026-11-09):** marginal signals from EIA-930 (F17). Novelty check: Sukprasert et al. 2024 already compare average vs marginal signals for shifting (65 regions); new here are the capped-facility setting, the swing law and siting under marginal signals. Raw EIA files are git-ignored (85 MB); the derived hourly signals (5.7 MB, public domain) are committed.
- **Iteration 9 (2026-11-09):** joint spatio-temporal shifting under caps (F18). Novelty check: the EuroSys'24 paper (Fig. 12) combines the two without capacity limits, and Hall et al. (2024) add both to Google's VCC framework, but neither quantifies how a cap makes them compete. The per-origin cumulative-flow LP is tested to reduce exactly to temporal-only and spatial-only shifting.
